# Mapping of Sea Ice Using the Nimbus 6 Satellite SCAMS Microwave Radiometer
### Final Project, 30350 Remote Sensing Fall 2025
Authors: Jenny Brun, Dahlia Dry, Hannah Gerlach

# Abstract
We analyze SCAMS microwave radiometer observations from March 1976 to evalu-
ate sea ice concentration (SIC) retrievals using both one-channel and two-channel algo-
rithms. A radiative transfer model (RTM) is applied to correct brightness temperatures,
enabling assessment of atmospheric effects on the retrievals. Sensitivity analysis shows
that cloud liquid water exerts by far the strongest influence on the 22 and 31 GHz chan-
nels, followed by water vapor, wind speed, and incidence angle, while sea surface tem-
perature and air temperature contribute only weakly in polar conditions. Applying the
correction generally increases SIC estimates, reduces brightness temperature variability,
and improves consistency between the one- and two-channel algorithms. The resulting
SIC fields and ice-type classifications reveal physically plausible patterns, with remaining
discrepancies likely linked to sampling limitations and model uncertainties.
